Myths 01–06 · Capability, correctness, safety
The mythThe reality
01

“You need PII to do anything useful.”

Most use cases do not. Hashed or masked identities are enough.

02

“The data would have to be properly structured first.”

Whatever humans do with our data today, AI does better.

03

“You’d need engineers.”

Replit and three hours of training lets anyone who can talk build.

04

“It’ll make calculation errors.”

It will, like a human. The difference is you train those out for good.

05

“It won’t be documented.”

Better documented than any other internal build.

06

“It won’t be safe.”

Standard Google Cloud environments and proper governance manage this.

The same six, in every function, every time. Worth answering once, properly.

09 / 14one.nz
Myths 07–12 · What it is, cost, decay, lock-in
The mythThe reality
07

“Replit is just a prototyping tool.”

It was, 12 months ago. It is anything but that now.

08

“We’ve got Claude Code, Codex and Pages — we don’t need Replit.”

They build prototypes and internal dashboards. Replit builds software.

09

“It’ll cost too much to build.”

NZ$10k in credits built the whole Workbench. Under $3k today with Claude Code.

10

“It’ll cost too much to run, and take too long to deliver.”

Most of it was built in three weeks between meetings. It runs on US$224–313 a month.

11

“It’ll go out of date and become a burden.”

It improves every day with more context, and every model upgrade lands free.

12

“We’d be locked into the platform, or the model.”

The opposite. The codebase is transferable; the model is a toggle.

Build cost is the author’s own figure. Run costs are actuals from the platform’s AI consumption logs, 4 Jun – 17 Aug 2026.

10 / 14one.nz
Not myths · the two that cost us

Two things I got wrong. That I’d like to put right.

Learning 01

Risk, privacy and architecture came in far too late

A proof of concept quietly became business-critical before anyone had the chance to understand or review it.

Learning 02

Define the measures before you build on them

AI will run a flawed method flawlessly and write it up persuasively. Agree what each measure means and how it’s calculated first — every AI analyst downstream inherits that definition, right or wrong.

Both are cheap to fix up front and expensive to fix later. The governance model on the next scene is built to force both.
