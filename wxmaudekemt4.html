The honest recipe

Six tools. Each earns its place.

The one that matters · Claude

Designs it, then builds it — and explains what it did in English you can check

Reads and changes a whole codebase coherently. The constraint was never coding ability — it was knowing which churn definition is the right one. That scarce input is yours, which is why this has to be you and not a project you commission.

~$200 a month to keep adding features

Wispr Flow · voice input

Not about speed — about context density. You speak far more context than you'd ever type.

3–5× context per instruction vs typing

Replit

App, database, storage, hosting, deploys. There is no infrastructure project — the reason business-led builds usually die on day one.

The bulk of the ~$10k build cost

GitHub

The undo button, the audit trail, and the thing that calms your security team down.

Every change arrives as a PR

Granola

Records and transcribes the room. The argument itself becomes the input — not somebody's recollection of it.

APIs

Claude, OpenAI, voice, email. Things you'd once have scoped as projects are now a key and an afternoon.

Everything I wish someone had told me first

Four pitfalls, honestly graded.

A wrong method, told persuasivelySingle-snapshot churn cuts produced a 28× “lock-in effect”, a 26% vs 3% split and a protective tenure story — none real

HappenedLikelihood

Banding fields get wiped on disconnect, so the rate was an artefact of the data model. The AI will never tell you your method is wrong — it will tell fifteen people your wrong answer in beautiful English. The fix is a written rule every analyst obeys.

You · method rigourBefore any churn cut ships

KPIs undefined before you build on themChurn computed three ways on three pages — all defensible, none reconcilable

LikelyLikelihood

Write down what each KPI means and exactly how it's calculated, then make that the single source the whole platform reads. Every analyst downstream inherits the definition — right or wrong, it propagates. The highest-return few hours in the build.

You · definitions docBefore the first report

Governance finds out after it mattersMy proof of concept quietly became business-critical before privacy, security and architecture knew

Near missLikelihood

Bring them in at week two, not month eight. Give them a document they can read in five minutes, name your own failure modes first, and agree the graduation trigger — the point where the PoC gets real engineering rigour — up front.

Privacy · Security · ArchWeek two

Starting with a platformThe most common way business-led builds fail

AvoidableLikelihood

Platforms accrete from useful things; they don't get designed in advance. Pick one report somebody reads on a Monday — ideally one that takes a day to produce — and use it for a fortnight before you build the second thing.

You · scopeBuild one, then wait
Cost me — wrong numbers reached decision-makers Trending badly unless you act early Watch only

What to actually do

Three commitments, this week.

01

Get the six licences — under your business entity

Replit, Claude, GitHub, Granola (or whatever meeting capture you're permitted), Wispr Flow, and an API key or two. Not personal accounts.

Why it's first

Retrofitting ownership later is painful, and it's the first thing finance and security will ask. Half a day, mostly waiting for approvals.

02

Pick one Monday report and prepare its inputs

One report somebody actually reads — ideally one that takes a day to produce. Identify the exports that feed it, confirm you can get them without raising a project, strip the PII, and write down your KPI definitions.

Why this shape

Not a platform. Platforms accrete from useful things. The KPI definitions are the highest-return few hours in the entire build.

And tell them what you're doing this week — not next quarter. Agree up front the point at which the build graduates from proof of concept to real engineering rigour.

Why now

This is the learning that nearly cost me. Naming the graduation trigger in advance is the single most reassuring thing you can offer a security function.

The close · time to ship each successive report

The first build is not the point.

Report 1Everything is new: data, definitions, conventions Weeks
Report 2The foundation and the definitions already exist Days
Report 3The conventions are settled; the memory is fuller A day
Report 5It starts from more knowledge than the one before A morning

Not because the tools improved. Because the context, the definitions, the conventions and the domain knowledge were already there. The curve only bends if you build in one place, against one memory.

Where that leaves you

Nobody's advantage is the data.

Every loyalty programme in this country has broadly the same data. The advantage is the accumulated institutional reasoning about it — the one asset no vendor can sell you and no competitor can copy, because it's made of your own arguments. You're not building software. You're building what your programme knows, in a form that doesn't leave when people do.
