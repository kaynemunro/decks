Act 1 · Why loyalty specifically

Loyalty isn't incidentally data-driven. It's structurally data-driven.

The whole discipline is four verbs — and every one of them is an analytics question before it is a marketing activity.

Optimise

Which offer, at which value, to which cohort?

Cross-sell

Who is ready, and what evidence says so?

Upsell

Where is the opportunity, and what are the best times to convert it?

Retain

Who is leaving, why, and what actually changes it?

Which means analytics quality is not a support function. It is the programme's operating capability.

Act 5 · Why voice, specifically

Voice isn't faster. It's denser.

The obvious reason to talk instead of type is the wrong one. You speak three to five times more than you'd type — and per Act 3, context is the whole game.

What you type

"Add a churn view cut by tenure band."

What you say

The same ask, plus why you want it, what you tried in 2024, which definition of churn you mean, who's going to read it, what it must not do, and the argument two people had about it in the room.

Then extend it: put six people in a room, record the whole disagreement, feed the disagreement in. No written brief has ever captured that.

Family A

"I haven't got what it takes."

Hands up if you've heard a version of this inside your own business in the last six months.

one.nz
Myth 01

"You need PII."

You don't. What you need is behaviour, tenure, plan, channel, spend band, engagement recency — almost none of which is identity. Masked or hashed keys carry the joins perfectly well.

And here's the part that matters strategically

The moment you drop identity from the dataset, the governance conversation changes character. It stops being "can we do this at all" and becomes "how do we control access to it" — a conversation your risk functions already know how to have.

one.nz
Myth 02 · the big one

"My data would have to be properly structured first."

This is the objection that stops most people before they start. It is also the one the technology has most comprehensively solved.

It does the joins

Across inconsistent exports with different keys and different naming.

It infers the taxonomy

Works out what the columns mean, and asks when it can't.

It reconciles the mess

The part that used to need an analyst and six weeks.

You are not data-poor. You are export-poor — and that's a five-minute problem, not a five-million-dollar one.

one.nz
Myth 03

"You'd need engineers."

Built single-handed, in spare time, by someone whose actual job is running a loyalty programme.

The constraint was never coding ability

It was knowing which churn definition is the right one. No engineer you hire will know that, and no consultant will learn it in the time you have. The scarce input is domain judgement — which is exactly why this has to be you, and not a project you commission.

one.nz
Family B

"It won't be trustworthy."

The most reasonable objection in the set — and the one where two of my answers are confessions rather than rebuttals.

one.nz
Myth 04

"It'll make calculation errors."

The opposite — and for reasons you can inspect rather than take on faith.

It narrates, it doesn't compute

The analysts are handed already-computed figures and asked to explain them. They never do their own arithmetic.

A deterministic check runs over every output

Not AI marking its own homework. Code pulls every percentage, dollar and count out of what the AI wrote and verifies each one against the source. Failures force a regeneration.

It shows its working, every time

Ask how it got there and it plays the method back. Try getting that out of a spreadsheet somebody built in 2022.

More reliable, more repeatable, and — the word that matters most — more investigatable.

one.nz
Learning · not a myth

It will not catch a wrong method. That one is on you.

We cut churn rates by customer attribute off a single snapshot. It produced three confidently wrong answers, and they reached decision-makers.

A 28× "lock-in" effect

That wasn't real.

A 26% vs 3% split

That wasn't real either.

Tenure looking protective

When it is, in fact, the opposite.

The cause wasn't the AI. The banding fields get wiped when a customer disconnects, so a single-snapshot rate is an artefact of the data model, not a finding. The fix wasn't a better model — it was a written rule that every analyst now obeys.

AI amplifies your rigour, or the absence of it. It will never tell you your method is wrong — but it will tell fifteen people your wrong answer, very persuasively, in beautiful English.

one.nz
Learning · not a myth

Define your KPIs before you build on them.

Write down what each one means and exactly how it's calculated — then make that definition the single source the whole platform reads.

Otherwise you get the classic failure

Churn computed three different ways on three different pages. All defensible. None reconcilable. And a room full of people arguing about which number is right instead of what to do about it.

It propagates either way

Every AI analyst downstream inherits your definition. Get it right once and that spreads. Get it wrong once and so does that.

A few hours of work, and the highest-return few hours in the entire build.

one.nz
Myth 05

"It won't be documented."

Better documented than most conventional internal builds — for a structural reason. The documentation is written as the work happens, not reconstructed afterwards.

Every change explains itself

Each one arrives as a pull request describing what it does and why. Code review is captured. The architecture is written down in plain English.

And the lessons are captured live

Eighty-odd hard-won production notes, written at the moment they were learned, while the detail was still fresh.

Which answers the question you're all about to ask: if I got hit by a bus tomorrow, the handover already exists.

one.nz
Myth 06

"It won't be safe."

Deployment is your choice

Private or public, per environment. Your call, not a vendor's.

It sits on standard cloud infrastructure

And passed our own security team's review.

With no PII, the risk surface is a different shape

Which is exactly why Myth 01 is worth the effort.

Then show the controls rather than asserting them

An email allowlist as the single gate. Magic-link authentication. Twelve permission groups. Every login and every page visit logged.

one.nz
The learning attached to that one

I brought them in far too late.

I stood up a proof of concept. It quietly became business-critical to the programme. And only then did privacy, security and architecture properly find out.

They were alarmed, and they were right to be — not because it was unsafe, but because they had no way to assess it and no hand in shaping it.

Bring them in at week two

Not month eight. Give them a document they can read in five minutes. Name your own failure modes before they do.

Agree the graduation trigger up front

At what point does this stop being a proof of concept and get real engineering rigour? Naming that in advance is the single most reassuring thing you can offer a security function.

Myth 10

"It'll go out of date and become a burden."

It gets better by being told things

Every AI analysis carries a Train AI button. You speak to the analyst exactly as you'd brief a junior team member — "say which window you measured, and stop calling it a lock-in effect" — and it applies from the next run on. No ticket. No release. No developer.

And every model upgrade is a free platform upgrade

When the underlying models improve their reasoning and their context windows, your platform gets better overnight without you doing anything.

This is the first category of software most people in this room will own that improves while they sleep. Conventional software decays from the day it ships.

Act 7 · Before you build anything

Five things, in this order.

1

Pick one report somebody reads on a Monday

Not a platform. One report — ideally one that currently takes a day to produce. Platforms accrete from useful things; they don't get designed in advance, and starting with a platform is the most common way this fails.

2

Identify the exports that feed it

Confirm you can get them without raising a project.

3

Strip the PII

Before anything goes anywhere.

4

Write down your KPI definitions

And exactly how each is calculated.

5

Name one ally each in privacy, security and architecture

And tell them what you're doing this week — not next quarter.

Then: record the conversation, voice the intent, read the brief and cut something, split the build, ship it — and use it for a fortnight before you build the second thing.

The first build is not the point.

Every report you add, every meeting you capture, every campaign review, every decision and its reasoning, every correction you give an analyst — it all lands in the same memory. So every feature after the first one starts from more knowledge than the one before it.

Where that leaves you

Nobody's advantage is the data.

Every loyalty programme in this country has broadly the same data. The advantage is the accumulated institutional reasoning about it — and that is the one asset no vendor can sell you and no competitor can copy, because it's made of your own arguments.

You're not building software. You're building what your programme knows — in a form that doesn't leave when people do.
