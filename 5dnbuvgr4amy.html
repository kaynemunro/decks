What you'll build · two shapes, pick one

Build a worker — or a reporter.

Two shapes of tool, same one-hour build. Pick a task from your own week, then decide which shape it is:

Option 1 · The worker

It does a task for you

  • Information goes in — pasted text, an upload, a form.
  • AI assesses it — against your rules, your policy, your context.
  • You eyeball the call — approve, reject, or nudge.
  • An action comes out — a drafted reply, a decision, something sent.
Complaint in → reply drafted for your sign-off
Meeting notes in → follow-up email you approve
Leave requests in → clash report
Option 2 · The reporter

It analyses and reports for you

  • Data goes in — a spreadsheet, weekly numbers, an export.
  • AI analyses it — trends, comparisons, the week-on-week story.
  • It flags what matters — anomalies, outliers, drafted commentary.
  • A report comes out — you edit the commentary and send it.
Weekly numbers in → commentary drafted, you edit
Invoices in → anomalies flagged for review
Sales data in → trend report, outliers named

Either way, the test is the same: it's repetitive — happens every week, roughly the same way. It needs your context — your rules and definitions, which is exactly why a plain chat window hasn't already solved it. And you check it before anything goes out.

Step 1 · before you hit record

Four things to talk through.

You're about to describe your tool out loud. Cover these four and the rest of the hour looks after itself:

01

The outcome

What does this tool produce when it works? A drafted email? A flag list? Be concrete about what "done" looks like.

02

How it knows it did well

What checks should it run on itself? What would make you distrust its answer? Say the failure cases out loud.

03

The inputs

What will you actually give it — pasted text, a file, a form? Describe what that input looks like, mess and all.

04

The work

What is the AI actually doing in the middle — assessing against what rules? And where exactly do YOU want to check before it acts?

Step 1 · 6 minutes · Granola

Hit record. Talk like a human.

Where to click · Granola desktop app
  1. Open Granola → click + New note, top right of the home screen.
  2. Glance at the transcript panel — words appearing as you talk means it's listening.
  3. Talk for ~6 minutes, then click End to stop the note.
  4. Open the note → select and copy the whole transcript — you're taking it to Claude next.

Mumble away

You don't need to speak clearly or in full sentences. Granola copes. Long pauses are fine too — think, then talk.

Change your mind mid-stream

"Actually, no, scrap that —" is valuable signal, not noise. The rethinking IS the requirements.

Don't dictate — ramble

You're not writing a document. You're thinking out loud about the four things on the last slide.

Then stop, and open the transcript

Six minutes is plenty. Copy the whole transcript — you're taking it to Claude next.

Step 2 · 8 minutes · Claude + Wispr Flow

Paste the transcript. Speak the instruction.

Where to click · claude.ai + Wispr Flow
  1. Go to claude.ai → start a New chat.
  2. Paste the whole Granola transcript into the message box.
  3. Press your Wispr Flow shortcut — the mic overlay appears wherever your cursor is.
  4. Speak the instruction below, release the shortcut, then send.
Say something like this — hit your Wispr shortcut and talk, don't type

I've pasted a transcript of me talking through a tool I want to build. I'm a business owner, not an engineer. Give me three things. One — a plain-English summary of the tool: the inputs, the work the AI does, the check I make in the middle, and the action that comes out the end. Under half a page, with anything that should wait for version two clearly parked. Two — a ready-to-paste build prompt for Replit Agent, describing the app screen by screen. The AI step must call the Anthropic API, and the key must be read from an environment secret called ANTHROPIC_API_KEY — never hard-coded. It needs a place where I approve or reject the AI's assessment before anything final happens. Three — a short design prompt I can run next to get the look and feel. If anything in my transcript is ambiguous, ask me up to three questions before you write anything.

Then cut something. Out loud. That single act is the entire job of a product owner, and it needs no technical skill at all.

Step 3 · 4 minutes · Claude

Look at it before you build it.

Next message — run the design prompt Claude just wrote you, or say this

Now show me the look and feel. Describe the layout — what sits at the top, the hierarchy of information, and where my approve/reject checkpoint lives — then draw it as simple wireframes so I can see it before we build anything. No code yet.

Ten seconds looking at a wireframe beats ten minutes rebuilding. This is the cheapest step in the hour and the one most people skip.

Step 4 · 12 minutes · Replit

Scaffold it. This is the long one.

Where to click · replit.com
  1. Go to replit.com → click Create App, top left of the home screen.
  2. Choose Describe an app — not a language template. You're telling it what you want in words.
  3. Paste the build prompt Claude wrote you in step 2 — the whole thing, untouched — then click Start building.
  4. Let it run. It takes a few minutes and narrates as it goes.

While it builds: reread your requirements

The plain-English summary Claude wrote you in step 2 is your checklist — you're about to check the agent's work against it.

Step 4 · still · Replit Preview

Preview is your app, live. Poke it.

Where to click · Replit workspace
  1. The Preview pane on the right is your app, running live. Click around in it like a user would.
  2. Spot something wrong? Type the fix as a plain sentence into the Agent chat on the left, and send.
  3. Repeat — poke, describe, let it fix — until your approve/reject checkpoint sits where you wanted it.

Use it like your user would

Paste a real-ish input in. Click the buttons. Does the checkpoint appear where you wanted it?

Every fix is a sentence

"The approve button should come BEFORE the email is drafted, not after."

Tell the agent what's wrong in plain English. That's the whole skill.

It will get at least one thing wrong. That's not failure — noticing it is the product owner's job, and the fix is never more than a sentence.

Step 5 · 4 minutes · Replit Secrets

The key goes in the vault. Never in the code.

Where to click · Replit → Tools → Secrets
  1. Get your key: console.anthropic.com → API Keys → copy the key you created before today.
  2. In Replit: open Tools in the left sidebar → click Secrets.
  3. Click + New Secret · Name: ANTHROPIC_API_KEY · Value: paste the key → save.
  4. Back in Preview, run the AI step. Your build prompt already told the agent to read this secret — it should work now.

This is why the secret exists: the key never appears in your code, so it never lands in GitHub, never shows in a screenshot, and can be revoked without touching the app. This is the pattern your security team will want to see.

Step 6 · 5 minutes · GitHub

Get your code out of Replit.

Where to click · Replit Git panel → GitHub
  1. Open the Git panel in Replit's left sidebar.
  2. Click Connect to GitHub and authorise the popup.
  3. Create a new repository — name it build-lab-yourname, set it to Public. That's fine: your key lives in Secrets, not code — just keep real customer data out of the repo.
  4. Click Commit & push, then go and look at your code on github.com.

This isn't bureaucracy. It's the undo button, the audit trail, and the single thing that will most calm your security team down. It's also what lets Claude work on it next.

Step 7 · 10 minutes · Claude Code

Now let Claude make a change.

Where to click · claude.ai/code
  1. Go to claude.ai/code — nothing to install, it runs in the browser.
  2. Click the repo & branch picker just above the message box → connect GitHub → choose your repo, branch main.
  3. Type (or Wispr) your one-sentence adjustment, and send.
  4. The first time it asks permission to edit or run something, choose Always allow — that's auto mode. It's a plain dialog; you can't miss it.
  5. When it finishes, click through to the pull request it opened on GitHub.
Pick well

Small, visible, one sentence

Good picks: a summary line above the result · a download button · a severity score on each assessment · a history of past runs.

Then read it

The pull request description — not the code

It tells you what it did and why, in English. Auto mode means you review once, at the end, instead of approving every step. That's the "it won't be documented" myth answering itself.

Step 8 · 6 minutes · GitHub + Replit

Merge it. Publish it. Open it on your phone.

Where to click · GitHub → Replit → your phone
  1. On your pull request page on GitHub, scroll to the bottom → click the green Merge pull request button. You can't miss it.
  2. Back in Replit: open the Git panel → click Pull to bring the merged change in.
  3. Click Publish, top right of the workspace (it reads Republish after your first deploy) → take the free option → wait for your URL.
  4. Get your phone out. Type the URL in. Look at it.

An hour ago this was you, talking. It's now a working tool with an AI step, a human checkpoint, and a URL you could send your team this afternoon.
